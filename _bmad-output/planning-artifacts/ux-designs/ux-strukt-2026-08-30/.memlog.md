- (note) PRD Vision (S1): strukt is a browser-based structural analysis tool built specifically for civil engineering students learning the direct stiffness method, and secondarily for engineers wanting a fast no-friction 2D check. Core loop: draw a beam/frame/truss on a canvas, assign supports and material/section properties, apply concentrated or uniformly distributed loads, click Solve, get instant BMD/SFD/NFD plus reactions.
- (note) PRD Vision (S1) differentiation thesis: the market splits between expensive dated professional tools (SAP2000, ETABS, STAAD.Pro, RISA-3D) that intimidate students, and free tools that are either code-first/inaccessible (OpenSees, Frame3DD) or bare-bones calculators with no real modeling workflow. Only SkyCiv courts students but its free tier is capped too tightly (5 elements/2 supports/3 loads). strukt's claimed gap: free-during-MVP + modern direct-manipulation canvas UI in the spirit of Figma + actually showing the stiffness-method equations behind a result -- nobody else combines all three.
- (note) PRD Vision (S1): strukt is explicitly a learning tool, not a substitute for licensed professional engineering judgment -- it will never claim to produce stamped or certified design output. Its bar for success is that a student trusts its diagrams enough to check their own hand calculations against it.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Node: a discrete point in the structure's geometry where Elements connect, Loads may be applied, and a Support may be assigned. Has coordinates (x, y) and degrees of freedom depending on Structure Type.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Element: a straight structural member connecting two Nodes (start, end), carrying a Material and Cross-Section.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Degree of Freedom (DOF): an independent direction of possible nodal displacement (translation or rotation) the solver solves for. Truss Nodes have 2 DOF (translation x, y); Frame Nodes have 3 DOF (translation x, y, rotation z).
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Support: a boundary condition assigned to a Node, constraining some or all of its DOF. Types: Fixed (all DOF restrained), Hinged (translations restrained, rotation free), Roller (one translation restrained), Free (no restraint). Note: the UI label is 'Hinged', not 'Pinned' -- see FR-4.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Direct Stiffness Method: the matrix structural analysis method strukt's engine uses to solve for nodal displacements/rotations and internal forces given geometry, Material/Cross-Section, Supports, and Loads.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Load: an applied force on the structure. v1 types: Concentrated Load (a single point force at a Node), Uniformly Distributed Load / UDL (constant-intensity force along an Element's length).
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Structure Type: Truss (Elements carry axial force only) or Frame (Elements carry axial, shear, and bending). Beam is a Frame whose Nodes are collinear/horizontal -- not a distinct Structure Type in the data model, just a UI preset (see FR-2).
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- BMD (Bending Moment Diagram): plot of internal bending moment along each Element.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- SFD (Shear Force Diagram): plot of internal shear force along each Element.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- NFD (Normal/Axial Force Diagram): plot of internal axial force along each Element.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Material: physical property set (elastic modulus E) assigned to an Element. v1: Steel, Concrete.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Cross-Section: geometric property set (area, moment of inertia) assigned to an Element, chosen from a catalog or entered manually.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Reaction: the force/moment the solver computes at a supported Node, balancing applied Loads.
- (note) Glossary (PRD S3) -- Project: a saved structure (all Nodes, Elements, Supports, Loads, Materials, Cross-Sections, and unit system) tied to a user account. Note: analysis results are NOT part of a Project -- reopening always shows the structure unsolved (see FR-23).
- (note) PRD 'Aesthetic and Tone' section (verbatim intent): Canvas-first UI inspired by Figma's direct-manipulation interaction model -- click/drag to place, a toolbar for tools, panels for properties -- but with a distinct visual identity, not a Figma reskin. Tone should feel approachable and modern, in deliberate contrast to the dated, intimidating UX of legacy engineering software (STAAD.Pro and ETABS are both widely criticized for this). The PRD explicitly hands the full visual system to bmad-ux -- this section exists to hand off intent, not specify pixels.
- (note) PRD 'Information Architecture' section (verbatim sketch, marked [ASSUMPTION: high-level only -- bmad-ux should own the real IA]): (1) a main canvas workspace for structure drawing/editing; (2) a toolbar/toolbox for adding Nodes, Elements, Supports, and Loads; (3) a properties panel for the currently selected Node/Element, covering Support type, Material, Cross-Section, and Load values; (4) a results view showing BMD/SFD/NFD and Reactions after solving; (5) a projects list/dashboard for browsing and reopening saved Projects, tied to accounts.
- (note) PRD UJ-1 'Sara checks her simply-supported beam homework' (full journey, S2.3): Persona/context -- Sara, a civil engineering student working through a structural-analysis homework problem on simply supported beams. Entry state -- signed in, on the main canvas workspace, starting a new project. Path -- she draws an 8m beam using snap-to-grid; opens the supports dropdown on each end node and sets both to Hinged; opens the Loads menu, selects Concentrated Load, and places three 5kN point loads along the beam; clicks Solve. Climax -- the engine solves instantly and renders the BMD, SFD, and NFD alongside the support reactions, so Sara can immediately compare peak values against her hand calculation. Resolution -- she trusts the result (or spots a mismatch and goes back to check her own math or her model), then saves the Project to her account so she can reopen it before her next study session (realizes FR-22).
- (note) PRD UJ-1 edge case (S2.3): if Sara's structure were actually unstable (e.g. she'd set a support to Free instead of Hinged/Roller/Fixed), strukt blocks solving with a specific, actionable error rather than showing a wrong or crashed result. This realizes FR-11 (instability detection).
- (note) FR-1 (PRD S4.1) 'Draw a structure on a snap-to-grid canvas', realizes UJ-1. Testable consequences: placed Nodes snap to the nearest grid point; an Element can only connect two existing Nodes; an Element cannot connect a Node to itself or to another Node at identical coordinates -- the canvas must block or merge these rather than letting the solver fail later on a divide-by-zero zero-length Element; drawing/selecting/editing must all work via touch input on a tablet, not just mouse -- tablet support is required for v1, not merely tolerated. PM note flags this as real added scope: touch-friendly hit targets, gesture handling for pan/zoom, and a properties panel layout that works without hover states.
- (note) FR-2 (PRD S4.1) 'Choose a Structure Type': user picks Truss or Frame for the project; Beam is offered as a Frame preset/template that constrains drawing to a single horizontal line of Nodes ([ASSUMPTION] Beam is a UI-level preset over Frame, not a new data model entity). Consequences: selecting 'Beam' constrains new Nodes to one horizontal line while the underlying stored Structure Type is Frame; selecting 'Truss' restricts Elements to axial-only behavior and hides the Cross-Section moment-of-inertia field (since the solver never uses it for Truss, avoiding a dead input); switching Structure Type after Elements/Supports/Loads already exist is blocked with a warning rather than silently carrying over now-partially-meaningless data. PM note: Show Steps (S4.7) must stay understandable to a student who picked 'Beam' even though the underlying matrices are Frame matrices -- a pedagogy-consistency risk detailed in addendum.md, explicitly flagged to resolve at UX time.
- (note) FR-3 (PRD S4.1) 'Edit and delete Nodes and Elements': user can select, move, and delete previously placed Nodes and Elements. Testable consequence: deleting a Node also removes any Elements attached to it, with a confirmation prompt -- this is the pattern FR-24 (delete Project) later matches for higher-stakes deletion.
- (note) FR-4 (PRD S4.2) 'Assign a Support to a Node': user assigns Fixed, Hinged, Roller, or Free to any Node via a dropdown menu; realizes UJ-1. Consequences: every Node defaults to Free until a Support is explicitly assigned; the dropdown label must read 'Hinged' (not 'Pinned') to match actual engineer/student vocabulary. PM note flags an unreconciled naming inconsistency in the existing codebase: engine/types.ts uses 'PINNED' while engine/constants.ts uses 'HINGE' -- architecture must reconcile the internal key, but the UI-facing label is already settled as 'Hinged'.
- (note) FR-5 (PRD S4.3) 'Assign a Material to an Element': user assigns Steel or Concrete to an Element. Consequences: an Element with no Material assigned blocks Solving (FR-10) with a specific validation error rather than silently defaulting to one Material; changing an Element's Material after a catalog Cross-Section (FR-6) was already chosen for it CLEARS that Cross-Section selection -- a Steel W-shape cannot remain attached to a Concrete Element (this is the material-switch invalidation behavior).
- (note) FR-6 (PRD S4.3) 'Choose a Cross-Section from a catalog': user picks a Cross-Section for an Element from a standard catalog (AISC W-shapes for steel; basic rectangular/circular sections for concrete), auto-filling area and moment of inertia -- this is v1's full catalog scope, decided. Consequence: AISC W-shapes are natively Imperial, so the catalog must convert cleanly into an SI-unit Project (FR-20) rather than showing a raw/awkward conversion -- the converted value may not exactly match a US Imperial textbook table, and the Correctness NFR's <=0.1% tolerance must account for this conversion step. Out of scope: a comprehensive multi-country/multi-standard section catalog beyond AISC W-shapes and basic concrete shapes.
- (note) FR-7 (PRD S4.3) 'Manually override Cross-Section properties': user can manually override the auto-filled area and/or moment of inertia for an Element. Consequences: manually overridden area/inertia must be positive numbers, else Solving (FR-10) is blocked with a validation error (to avoid a singular/nonsensical result indistinguishable from a genuine instability); selecting a new catalog Cross-Section after a manual override REPLACES the override with the catalog's values -- the override never silently persists alongside a newly-displayed catalog shape name.
- (note) FR-8 (PRD S4.4) 'Apply a Concentrated Load': user applies a Concentrated Load to any Node, specifying magnitude and direction; realizes UJ-1. Consequences: direction is specified relative to a global coordinate system, not implicitly always-downward ([ASSUMPTION] exact sign convention, e.g. positive y = upward, deferred to architecture), and Show Steps (FR-17) must reflect whatever convention is used; multiple Concentrated Loads may be applied to the same Node and their effects sum rather than one overwriting another.
- (note) FR-9 (PRD S4.4) 'Apply a Uniformly Distributed Load (UDL)': user applies a UDL along any Element, specifying intensity. Consequences: intensity/direction follow the same global-coordinate-system convention as Concentrated Loads (FR-8); multiple UDLs may be applied to the same Element and their effects sum rather than one overwriting another. Out of scope for v1: trapezoidal/varying loads, applied moments, support settlements.
- (note) FR-10 (PRD S4.5) 'Solve the structure', realizes UJ-1 (the Solve button/action). Consequences with direct UX implications: Solve completes for a classroom-scale structure (~50 Nodes/Elements) in under 1 second end-to-end including diagram render; beyond ~50 Nodes/Elements strukt does not block solving but makes no performance/accuracy guarantee, degrading rather than crashing or silently producing a wrong answer; ANY structural edit (Node, Element, Support, Load, Material, or Cross-Section change) made after a Solve invalidates the displayed results (BMD/SFD/NFD, Reactions, and Show Steps) until the user solves again -- stale results are never left visible against an edited structure (this is the stale-results invalidation rule, also realizes FR-16).
- (note) FR-11 (PRD S4.5) 'Detect and block unstable structures', realizes UJ-1's edge case. System detects instability (insufficient restraint; singular/near-singular global stiffness matrix) at or before solve time and blocks solving with a specific, actionable error. Consequences: an unstable structure never returns a numeric result silently -- always an explicit instability error, never a crash or garbage output; the error message identifies the specific Node(s) or region responsible where feasible (e.g. 'Node N3 is unrestrained'), not just a generic 'structure is unstable'; if a Project has multiple disconnected sub-structures (no shared Node), instability is checked per connected sub-structure and the error identifies which sub-structure is unstable (v1 non-goal: solving the valid island independently -- v1 blocks the whole Project's solve); a structure with zero Elements (only Nodes placed) gets a distinct 'nothing to analyze yet' message, not the same wording as a geometric instability error.
- (note) FR-12 (PRD S4.6) 'Render the Bending Moment Diagram' for a solved Frame/Beam structure, realizes UJ-1. Consequence: the diagram labels the peak positive and peak negative moment values and their location along each Element. Rendering only occurs after a successful Solve (FR-10) with no blocking FR-11 error.
- (note) FR-13 (PRD S4.6) 'Render the Shear Force Diagram' for a solved Frame/Beam structure, realizes UJ-1. Consequence: the diagram labels the peak positive and peak negative shear values and their location along each Element.
- (note) FR-14 (PRD S4.6) 'Render the Normal/Axial Force Diagram' for a solved structure's Elements, realizes UJ-1. Consequence: the diagram must distinguish tension from compression (e.g. via sign or color), matching whatever convention Show Steps (FR-17) also uses.
- (note) FR-15 (PRD S4.6) 'Display Reactions' at every supported Node after solving, realizes UJ-1. Consequence: displayed Reactions must satisfy global equilibrium with the applied Loads (within the <=0.1% Correctness NFR tolerance) -- an implicit, testable correctness signal, not just a UI requirement.
- (note) FR-16 (PRD S4.7) 'Toggle Show Steps': user toggles a 'Show Steps' view alongside solved results -- a key differentiator per the Vision, off by default so the fast path (UJ-1) stays fast. Consequences: results (BMD/SFD/NFD/Reactions) are visible by default without toggling anything; Show Steps is opt-in, never forced; the toggle state is scoped to the current solve -- reopening a Project defaults back to results-only; Show Steps is DISABLED (not just empty) until at least one successful Solve has occurred in the current session; if a subsequent Solve attempt is blocked by an FR-11 instability error, any previously-shown results AND Show Steps content are CLEARED, not left visible, since they described a structure since edited into an unstable one.
- (note) FR-17 (PRD S4.7) 'Show each Element's local stiffness matrix': Show Steps displays, per Element, the local stiffness matrix formula with that Element's actual material, cross-section, and geometry values substituted in -- not just the resulting numeric matrix.
- (note) FR-18 (PRD S4.7) 'Show global stiffness matrix assembly': Show Steps displays the DOF mapping from each Element's local stiffness matrix into the global stiffness matrix, and the assembled global matrix itself.
- (note) FR-19 (PRD S4.7) 'Show the boundary-condition-reduced solve': Show Steps displays which DOFs were eliminated by Supports, the reduced system of equations actually solved, and how it yields the nodal displacements/rotations shown in the results. Feature-specific NFR: whatever Show Steps displays must exactly match the values the solver actually used (FR-10) -- a wrong or fabricated 'step' is worse than not showing steps at all, since the feature's entire premise is being trustworthy to learn from.
- (note) FR-20 (PRD S4.8) 'Select unit system': user selects SI or Imperial per project; all geometry, Load, and result values display and accept input in the selected system. Consequences: switching unit system converts DISPLAYED values without altering the underlying stored structure -- the canonical stored value is authoritative, so repeated round-trip conversion (Imperial to SI to Imperial) never drifts the displayed value from what the user originally entered; unit system is changeable at ANY point in a Project's life, not fixed at creation (mid-project mutable).
- (note) FR-21 (PRD S4.9) 'Create an account and sign in': user can create an account and sign in via email/password or social login (e.g. Google). [ASSUMPTION] specific auth provider/vendor not chosen, deferred to architecture -- but the sign-in methods themselves (email/password + social login) are decided at PRD level.
- (note) FR-22 (PRD S4.9) 'Save a Project': signed-in user saves the current structure as a named Project tied to their account. Consequence: Project names are unique per account -- saving with a name already in use PROMPTS the user to confirm overwrite or choose a different name; it never silently overwrites.
- (note) FR-23 (PRD S4.9) 'Load a Project': signed-in user views a list of their saved Projects and reopens (loads) any of them. Consequence: reopening a Project exactly reproduces the saved Nodes, Elements, Supports, Loads, Materials, Cross-Sections, and unit system -- but analysis results are NOT part of a Project, so reopening always shows the structure unsolved (a 'solve to see results' state), never stale results from before the save.
- (note) FR-24 (PRD S4.9) 'Delete a Project': signed-in user deletes a saved Project. Consequence: deletion requires a confirmation prompt, matching the pattern FR-3 already sets for the lower-stakes Node/Element deletion.
- (note) PRD Non-Goals (S5) + Constraints/Safety: strukt is not a substitute for stamped/certified professional structural design work -- it is a learning tool, and per the Safety constraint this must be communicated explicitly IN-PRODUCT (not just in the PRD), persistently. The line drawn: engineers may use strukt for informal personal checks (refreshing a concept, sanity-checking intuition) but never for anything informing a real deliverable, client conversation, or professional judgment call on an actual project.
- (note) PRD Platform section + S5/S6.2 Non-Goals: strukt is web-only, browser-based, built on the existing Next.js/React stack. The drawing canvas supports BOTH mouse and tablet/touch input (FR-1) -- it is explicitly not desktop-only -- but there is no dedicated native mobile app in v1, and phone-sized screens are not a target form factor.
- (note) PRD Monetization section + counter-metric SM-C2: strukt has no monetization during the MVP phase -- entirely free (S5, S6.1). A 'grandfather commitment' guarantees any feature free during the MVP phase stays free forever for users who already had access before paid tiers launch -- strukt will not paywall something an existing user already relies on, explicitly to avoid the same free-tier-erosion criticism the Vision levels at SkyCiv. SM-C2 also blocks growing WAU during the MVP phase via professional/paid-leaning features that dilute the free-for-students positioning. Together these bound UX away from any dark-pattern monetization pressure in v1.

Explicitly left for bmad-ux or architecture to resolve, not decided at PRD level.
- (note) Addendum 'Known edge cases deferred to UX/architecture' (verbatim item): Working on the canvas before signing in (FR-1 vs. FR-21) -- is drawing allowed anonymously at all, and if so, does triggering sign-in to Save (FR-22) preserve the in-progress canvas across the auth redirect, or discard it? Explicitly left for bmad-ux or architecture to resolve, not decided at PRD level.
- (note) Addendum 'Known edge cases deferred to UX/architecture' (verbatim item): Cross-Section catalog value corrected/updated after a Project was saved using the old value (FR-6/FR-22) -- does a Project snapshot the resolved numeric Area/Inertia at save time (reproducible, but can silently diverge from a 'live' catalog), or reference the catalog live (reproducible against the catalog, but can silently change a previously-solved Project's results on reopen)? Addendum calls this a real trust/correctness trade-off, not cosmetic -- whoever resolves it should read the addendum note first.
- (note) Addendum 'Competitive Landscape' -- the gap strukt targets: nobody combines (a) genuinely free-during-MVP access for students, (b) a modern, direct-manipulation canvas UI, and (c) a browser-native Direct Stiffness Method engine with teaching-oriented UX. Methodology caveat: this is same-day desk research (pricing pages, comparison sites, forum threads), not user interviews or click-through testing -- it establishes the conjunction is unclaimed, not that users actually choose tools along that conjunction. Treat as a validated gap, not a validated thesis.
- (note) Addendum 'Competitive Landscape' -- legacy tool UX contrast basis: STAAD.Pro (Bentley, ~$5,600/license) is frequently criticized for a 'frozen in time' GUI and steep learning curve; RISA-3D is easier to pick up than STAAD but its pricing 'has gotten absurd' per user complaints; SAP2000/ETABS are desktop, paid (~$1000s), professional-grade and relatively more beginner-friendly than peers but still dated. This is the concrete basis for the PRD's Aesthetic and Tone claim of deliberate contrast against dated, intimidating legacy engineering software UX.
- (note) Addendum 'Competitive Landscape' -- closest technical/UX analogs: SkyCiv is 100% browser-based and the only competitor with a deliberate student program, but its free tier is capped too tightly (5 elements/2 supports/3 loads) for real coursework. Stabileo (open-source, WASM in-browser) is the closest direct technical analog -- 2D/3D structural analysis in-browser via Direct Stiffness Method, real-time solving -- but does not appear to have a Figma-style canvas UX, meaning strukt's visual/interaction differentiation is not yet claimed by the one project closest on technical architecture.
