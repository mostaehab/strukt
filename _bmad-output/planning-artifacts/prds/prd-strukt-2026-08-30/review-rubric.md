# PRD Quality Review — strukt

## Overall verdict

This PRD is well-formed at the strategic layer — it has a real, specific thesis (student-first, free, teaching-oriented, canvas-native), the two Discovery reversals (Show Your Work into scope; "free forever" refined to "free during MVP") are grounded and mostly threaded through consistently, and its brownfield claims about the existing codebase check out exactly against the actual files. It is weakest at the layer downstream work leans on hardest: roughly half the FRs (materials, loads, and — most importantly — the four Results Visualization FRs that render the product's actual deliverable) have no stated testable consequence, and three cross-references to "§8 Open Question 5/8/9" point at open questions that no longer exist after consolidation. Neither defect is large to fix, but both would cost a downstream engineer or story-writer real time before they hit the gap.

## Decision-readiness — strong

Trade-offs are surfaced rather than smoothed. The addendum documents the Show Your Work reversal as an explicit weighing ("the teaching value of showing real equations to students outweighs the added MVP scope," *addendum §"Show your work"*) rather than presenting it as an uncontested improvement, and the Monetization section is honest that gating/pricing is undecided by design rather than by oversight (§ Monetization, `[NOTE FOR PM]`). `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land at real tensions, not safe checkpoints: FR-1's tablet requirement is flagged as "real added scope for canvas interaction design" (§4.1), and FR-4's naming callout surfaces an actual unresolved conflict between `engine/types.ts` and `engine/constants.ts` rather than restating a decision already made. Open Questions (§8) are genuinely open (auth vendor, typesetting library, paid-plan shape) — none are rhetorical. No instance of the "everything is important / every choice balances everything" red flag was found; the Correctness NFR is explicitly singled out as "the single most important quality bar," not one of several equally-weighted concerns.

No findings — this dimension needs none.

## Substance over theater — strong

One primary persona (Sara), driving nearly every FR via "Realizes UJ-1" tags — not persona theater. The Vision (§1) is specific enough that it could not be swapped into another PRD: it names four priced competitors, cites SkyCiv's exact free-tier caps ("5 elements / 2 supports / 3 loads"), and stakes a specific bar for success ("a student trusts its diagrams enough to check their own hand calculations against it"). The differentiation claim is evidenced, not asserted — the addendum's Competitive Landscape digest is a real survey (SAP2000/ETABS/STAAD.Pro/RISA-3D/SkyCiv/ClearCalcs/OpenSees/Stabileo, with prices and specific criticisms), and it explicitly names the one close technical analog (Stabileo) rather than claiming an empty field. NFRs carry actual thresholds (≤0.1% relative error, <1s for ~50 nodes) instead of "must be scalable/secure" boilerplate.

No findings — this dimension needs none.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated once and then followed: "nobody combines a genuinely free, student-first offering with a modern, direct-manipulation canvas UI... and none of them show the actual stiffness-method equations behind a result" (§1). Feature prioritization follows from it — Show Your Work was pulled forward specifically because it serves the thesis (addendum), not because it was easy. Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than just measuring activity: SM-5 (Show Steps adoption, target >30%) directly tests whether the stated differentiator is landing, and SM-1 uses WAU (matching homework cadence) rather than a DAU vanity metric. Counter-metrics are present for every metric that could be gamed (SM-C1–C3), including one that explicitly protects the free-during-MVP positioning from feature creep (SM-C2). MVP scope reads as thesis-driven (core loop + one differentiator + validation suite), not a backlog with headings.

No findings — this dimension needs none.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

The best-specified FRs are very good — FR-10, FR-11, and FR-16–19 give an engineer a real testable target, down to "matching known solutions within ≤0.1% relative error" and "Solve completes... in under 1 second, end-to-end including diagram render" (§4.5). But roughly half the FRs — 5, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 14, 15, 21, 22, 23, 24 — carry no "Consequences (testable)" block and no equivalent testable detail folded into the FR body. This is the dimension the rubric asks to be unforgiving on, and the gaps land in load-bearing places.

### Findings

- **high** Results Visualization FRs have no testable consequences (§4.6, FR-12 "System renders the BMD for a solved Frame/Beam structure," FR-13, FR-14, FR-15) — these four FRs render the actual deliverable of the product (per §1 Vision, the diagrams *are* the product), yet nothing states sign convention, axis/scale behavior, whether peak values are annotated, or what "solved" gates rendering. An engineer building this has only a one-line description to work from. *Fix:* add Consequences to each — e.g. "BMD sign convention is [X]; the diagram labels the peak positive/negative moment value and its location."
- **high** Load FRs have no direction/sign convention stated (§4.4, FR-8 Concentrated Load "specifying magnitude and direction," FR-9 UDL "specifying intensity") — for a solver whose top NFR is correctness (≤0.1% error), the absence of a stated sign/direction convention for the two load types is a correctness-adjacent gap, not just a UX one. *Fix:* state the convention (e.g. global vs. local axes, positive-direction definition) as a testable consequence.
- **medium** Material and Cross-Section FRs lack testable consequences (§4.3, FR-5 Assign Material, FR-7 Manual override) — no stated default when no Material is assigned, no statement of whether Solve (FR-10) is blocked without one, and no validation rule for manually overridden area/inertia (e.g., must be positive). *Fix:* add consequences analogous to FR-4's "defaults to Free until assigned" pattern.
- **medium** Accounts/Projects FRs lack testable consequences, and FR-24 breaks a pattern FR-3 already set (§4.9, FR-21–FR-24) — FR-3 (delete a Node) explicitly requires "a confirmation prompt" for a destructive action; FR-24 (delete a Project, an equally destructive and arguably higher-stakes action) states none. Save/Load (FR-22/23) also have no stated round-trip invariant (e.g., does a reloaded Project reproduce the exact geometry/loads/unit system saved). *Fix:* extend the FR-3 confirmation pattern to FR-24; add a round-trip consequence to FR-22/23.
- **low** The Performance NFR's precision is undercut by a soft qualifier — "on typical student hardware/browsers" (Cross-Cutting NFRs) sits next to an otherwise hard bound (<1s, ~50 Nodes/Elements). Minor, but it's exactly the kind of adjective the rubric flags. *Fix:* either drop the qualifier or define "typical" (e.g., a specific device/browser tier).

## Scope honesty — adequate

The Non-Goals section (§5) does real work and the two Discovery reversals are each documented with their own rationale rather than left to silent inference. But two specific spots don't carry a scope decision consistently to every place it appears.

### Findings

- **medium** FR-6's catalog scope is asserted as decided in the PRD but reopened in the addendum. §4.3 FR-6 states plainly: "The catalog covers AISC W-shapes (steel) and basic rectangular/circular sections (concrete) — **this is v1's full catalog scope, decided**." The addendum's own section titled "Section-catalog scope (FR-6) — technical-how, not decided" then treats the identical specifics as merely illustrative: "Not yet decided which standard(s) v1's Cross-Section catalog draws from (e.g. AISC W-shapes for steel...)." One of these two framings is wrong, and a reader of only one document will come away with the opposite belief about whether this is settled. *Fix:* pick one — either FR-6's "decided" language should soften to match the addendum, or the addendum section should be updated/removed to match FR-6.
- **medium** The "free forever → free during MVP" reversal is carefully scoped everywhere it's operationally load-bearing (§5 Non-Goals: "this Non-Goal applies to v1, not to strukt's lifetime"; §6.2; § Monetization; SM-C2's explicit reconciliation) but the top-line Vision (§1) and the addendum's Competitive Landscape framing both use unqualified language: "nobody combines a **genuinely free**, student-first offering..." (§1) and "strukt targets... (a) **genuinely free** access for students" (addendum). Per dimension 6's "each section should make sense pulled out alone" — a reader of §1 alone (the section most likely to be quoted externally, e.g. in marketing) has no signal that "free" is MVP-phase-scoped, not permanent. *Fix:* add the same MVP-phase qualifier to the Vision's "free" claim, or add a forward pointer to § Monetization.

## Downstream usability — adequate

Glossary terms (Node, Element, Support, DOF, Load, Structure Type, BMD/SFD/NFD, Material, Cross-Section, Reaction, Project) are capitalized and used identically everywhere they recur, including in the four FRs that lack Consequences blocks. FR IDs (1–24), SM IDs (1–5 + 3 counter-metrics), and the single UJ (UJ-1) are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates. The Assumptions Index (§9) round-trips cleanly — all six inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (FR-2, FR-21, Cross-Cutting NFRs, Constraints ×2, Information Architecture) are indexed, and the index has no orphan entries. The one real defect is a set of broken cross-references, flagged below.

### Findings

- **high** Three cross-references point at Open Questions that don't exist. §8 Open Questions lists exactly three items, numbered 1–3, and its own intro states "All prior open questions were resolved in a single pass... What's left..." implying a deliberate renumbering/consolidation happened. But: § Monetization says "Revisit... (see §8 Open Question **9**)"; the addendum's "Show your work" section says typesetting is "left for architecture to decide (§8 Open Question **8**)"; the addendum's "Section-catalog scope (FR-6)" section says "See PRD §8 Open Question **5**." None of 5, 8, or 9 exist in the current §8 — the actual answers those numbers were pointing to (paid-plan shape, typesetting, catalog standard) are present in §8 items 1–3 and elsewhere, just under different numbers, or — in the FR-6 catalog-standard case — not represented in §8 at all. This is exactly the kind of defect that costs a downstream workflow real time chasing a reference that resolves to nothing. *Fix:* renumber the three cross-references to match the current §8 (items 1–3), and either add a fourth §8 item for the FR-6 catalog-standard question or point that reference at the existing addendum section directly instead of a non-existent §8 number.

## Shape fit — adequate

This is correctly shaped as a consumer product with a load-bearing named UJ (Sara, §2.3) — not over-formalized with a UJ-per-feature pattern, not under-formalized given the single core loop the product centers on. The brownfield handling is a genuine strength: every specific claim in the addendum's "Existing codebase" section was checked directly against the repository and is accurate — `engine/types.ts`'s `StructuralSupport` really does use `"PINNED"` while `engine/constants.ts`'s `SUPPORTS` really does use `"HINGE"` for the same concept (confirming FR-4's `[NOTE FOR PM]`); `engine/stiffness.ts` really does contain only two empty-bodied stub functions; `store/useStructureStore.ts`'s CRUD shape matches the addendum's description exactly; `three` (^0.184.0) and `mathjs` (^15.2.0) are both real dependencies in `package.json`, matching the 3D-deferral and client-side-solver claims; and `app/page.tsx` is confirmed to be the unedited `create-next-app` scaffold. Brownfield references here are trustworthy, not aspirational.

### Findings

- **medium** Accounts & Projects (§4.9, FR-21–FR-24) — a full feature area, four FRs — has no UJ coverage at all, and the secondary engineer persona (§2.1, JTBD bullet 4) similarly has no UJ. UJ-1 covers only the modeling→solve→results loop. This is defensible for a lean MVP UJ set, but it means a UX designer has no narrative to design the sign-up/save/load flow against, only the bare FR list (which — per the Done-ness findings above — also lacks testable consequences for this area). *Fix:* either fold an account/project beat into UJ-1 (e.g., Sara returns to reopen a saved Project) or add a short UJ-2 for it.

## Mechanical notes

Glossary usage is clean throughout — no case/plural/synonym drift found across FRs, UJ-1, or Success Metrics. ID continuity is intact: FR-1–24 contiguous with no gaps or duplicates, SM-1–5 plus SM-C1–3 contiguous, and the single UJ-1 is referenced consistently rather than left floating. The Assumptions Index (§9) has full roundtrip integrity — every inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag (FR-2, FR-21, Cross-Cutting NFRs' browser-compatibility line, both Constraints and Guardrails tags, and Information Architecture) is indexed, and every index entry traces back to an inline tag. The one broken-cross-reference cluster is detailed under Downstream usability above (§8 Open Question 5/8/9 pointing at nothing) — treat that as the one mechanical item actually worth fixing before this PRD is used as a source document for UX/architecture/epics work.
