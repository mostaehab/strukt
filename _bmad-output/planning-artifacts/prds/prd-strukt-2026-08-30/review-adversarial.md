# Adversarial Review: strukt PRD (2026-08-30)

This is not a summary and not a balanced assessment. It is a hunt for the weakest claims in this document, on the assumption that a skeptical stakeholder will find them anyway — better here than in the room.

## 1. Competitive positioning is a checklist conjunction, not evidence of demand

The Vision (§1) and the addendum's "gap strukt targets" both argue uniqueness by combining three dimensions — (a) free, (b) modern canvas UI, (c) browser-native DSM engine with teaching UX — and noting that no single competitor has all three. This is differentiation-by-conjunction: stack enough axes and almost anything becomes "unique." It says nothing about whether users select tools on that specific conjunction, or whether any one of the three axes is actually load-bearing for the JTBDs in §2.1.

- "Free" is not scarce. The addendum's own digest lists nine free browser calculators (StructureCalcs, Beamsolver, Optimal Beam, STRIAN, Truzme, BeamGuru, CivilCalc, FEA-Apps, EasyCiv) already serving "quick homework-style checks." The document dismisses these as lacking "a full modeling workflow" without ever establishing that JTBD-1 (checking homework) actually requires a full modeling workflow rather than a faster form-based calculator. A canvas where you place nodes, snap to grid, open dropdowns per node to assign supports, then open a Loads menu to place three point loads (UJ-1) is plausibly *more* clicks than typing three numbers into a homework calculator's form. Nothing in the PRD tests or even asserts that canvas-based modeling is lower-friction than the "bare-bones" competitors it's positioned against — it just asserts the competitors are worse without comparing task time or click count.
- The Stabileo comparison ("closest technical analog... does not appear to have a Figma-style canvas UX") is hedged in the addendum with "does not appear" — a researcher's guess, not a verified absence — yet this hedge is laundered into an unhedged claim in the PRD body: "Nobody combines a genuinely free, student-first offering with a modern, direct-manipulation canvas UI... strukt fills that gap." An open-source project with a working DSM engine already in the browser is one fork away from closing the one gap the vision leans on hardest, and the PRD gives it a full paragraph of free due diligence on how to do exactly that.
- SkyCiv is stated as already having "a deliberate student program (discounted subscription + institutional licenses)" — i.e., it already targets students seriously. strukt's entire edge over the one competitor who's already doing this is "still cheaper" (free vs $19.99/mo) plus a UI bet. That's a price-and-polish wager against an incumbent with revenue, support staff, and an existing student funnel — not a JTBD-level insight competitors have missed.
- ClearCalcs (paid, $79/mo, targets *working engineers*, sells "transparent calc reports") is cited in the same competitive digest and directly undercuts the JTBD-4 premise that engineers are blocked by cost from getting a fast/transparent check — there's a whole paid category serving exactly "transparent calc output for engineers" at real prices, meaning "free" isn't obviously the binding constraint for that persona either.

**Bottom line:** the positioning is asserted, not evidenced. No user interviews, no click-through comparison, no willingness-to-pay or willingness-to-switch data appears anywhere in the PRD or addendum to support "will actually convert users away from free calculators or SkyCiv." It reads as a plausible story, not a validated thesis.

## 2. This is not an MVP — it's a v1.0 commercial product wearing an MVP badge

Look at what "In Scope" (§6.1) actually demands, starting from a codebase the PRD itself describes as "mostly stubbed" (§0) — empty solver stubs (`engine/stiffness.ts`), empty UI directories (`components/canvas/`, `components/panels/`), and an unedited Next.js scaffold (addendum, "Existing codebase"):

- A full Direct Stiffness Method solver for both Truss (2 DOF) and Frame (3 DOF) elements, with instability detection via singular-matrix analysis.
- A maintained, versioned regression/validation benchmark suite matched to ≤0.1% relative error — this is not a checkbox, it's an ongoing QA engineering commitment with no named owner, no problem count, and no maintenance process specified anywhere.
- A snap-to-grid direct-manipulation canvas with full mouse AND touch/tablet parity — the PRD's own inline note under FR-1 admits this is "real added scope... touch-friendly hit targets, gesture handling for pan/zoom, and a properties panel layout that works without hover states" — and it's still in scope, with zero JTBD ever mentioning a tablet as a device the persona uses.
- A materials/cross-section system with a real engineering data catalog (AISC W-shapes, concrete sections) that needs to be sourced or licensed, per the addendum's own "not yet decided" flag on FR-6.
- Two load types with a full property/edit UI.
- Three diagram renderers (BMD/SFD/NFD) plus reactions display.
- "Show Your Work" — not a nice-to-have caption, but three distinct sub-features (FR-17/18/19) requiring the solver to expose and the UI to typeset per-element stiffness matrices, DOF mapping, global assembly, and a boundary-condition-reduced system of equations, matched exactly to what the solver actually computed. This alone is a UI+solver-integration project comparable in complexity to the rest of the modeling app combined, and its own typesetting approach (KaTeX/MathJax/custom) isn't even decided yet.
- Dual unit systems (SI + Imperial) with full round-trip conversion across geometry, loads, and results, without ever clarifying whether the cross-section catalog itself is unit-aware (AISC W-shapes are natively imperial-labeled) — a real spec gap masquerading as a settled FR.
- Full account system: signup, signin, email/password AND social login, multi-project CRUD, cloud persistence, account deletion semantics.

There is no phased rollout, no "walking skeleton" cut, and no explicit acknowledgment anywhere in §6 that this list, taken together, is enormous for a product with zero existing UI and zero existing solver logic. Compare to the Non-Goals (§5), which read like the *actual* MVP boundary was drawn around "not 3D, not dynamic analysis, not code-checking" — i.e., the cuts are all *future* features, not cuts to the v1 list itself. An MVP that cuts nothing from its own feature list except things nobody was going to ship anyway is not minimum, it's just "everything, minus the stuff too big to build."

If accounts, tablet support, dual units, and Show Your Work all shipped a release behind the core draw→load→solve→diagram loop, nothing about the differentiation thesis in §1 would be falsified any earlier or later than it will be now — which suggests they're in v1 by inertia/scope-creep-avoidance, not by necessity.

## 3. Success Metrics measure activity, not the thesis — and the one metric that would test it is buried as secondary

- **SM-1 (WAU)** and **SM-2 (solve success rate)** are the two Primary metrics. Both can hit target in a world where strukt is used purely as "yet another free calculator" — nobody touches Show Steps, nobody notices or cares about the canvas UI's Figma-inspired polish, the differentiation thesis in §1 is completely absent from the actual usage pattern — and the PRD would still call this a launch success. SM-2 in particular is a reliability/QA metric (crash rate, essentially) dressed up as a growth-validating "Primary" metric; it validates that the team didn't ship broken software, not that the team built the right thing.
- **SM-4 (solver accuracy)** is a launch gate, not a success metric — of course you must pass your own benchmark suite before shipping a "trustworthy" solver. Listing it as a Secondary *success* metric conflates "did QA pass" with "did the product succeed." A 100% target against an unspecified, unsized, presumably author-curated benchmark suite (see Finding 2) is also a metric that can't fail by construction — nobody publishes a benchmark suite designed to be failed.
- **SM-5 (Show Steps adoption, >30%)** is the *only* metric in §7 that actually tests whether "strukt's core differentiator is actually landing" — its own description says so explicitly. And it's Secondary. The PRD's Vision (§1) calls trust-in-diagrams the bar for success and the addendum calls Show Your Work "a real differentiator... a genuine point of difference" — yet the metric structure lets the product be declared successful (SM-1 + SM-2 green) while this one honestly-diagnostic metric sits anywhere from 0% to 30%, unmeasured against any gate. If the actual differentiator is optional and its adoption is secondary, the primary metrics are measuring a generic calculator's success, not strukt's.
- **"Trust"** is stated as the literal bar for success in §1 ("a student trusts its diagrams enough to check their own hand calculations against it") but no metric in §7 measures trust, perceived credibility, or even repeat comparison behavior. The closest proxies (SM-2 crash rate, SM-4 accuracy) measure whether the solver is *actually* correct, which is necessary but not sufficient for whether students *believe* it's correct — the PRD's own stated success bar is unmeasured.

## 4. SM-C2 vs. planned paid tiers: a counter-metric with no exit criterion, guarding a boundary nobody has drawn

SM-C2 says: don't grow WAU during the MVP phase by adding professional/paid-leaning features that dilute free-for-students positioning — but this "applies to the MVP phase specifically" and is explicitly stated not to conflict with the planned post-MVP paid plans. Two problems:

1. **"MVP phase" has no defined end.** §8 Open Question 3 and the Monetization section both defer the paid-plan shape "until the MVP phase is ending" — but nothing in the document defines what event, date, or metric threshold marks that boundary. A counter-metric scoped to an undefined phase is unfalsifiable: you cannot know, at any given moment, whether SM-C2 currently applies. This isn't a minor gap — it's the exact mechanism meant to protect the free positioning, and it has no trigger condition.
2. **The bait-and-switch risk is real and undiscussed.** The PRD explicitly criticizes SkyCiv for a free tier "capped too tightly for real coursework" as evidence of a market gap — but nothing in this document commits to *not* doing exactly that to strukt's own MVP users once paid tiers launch. Will existing free users be grandfathered on every MVP-era feature (including Show Your Work, cloud saves, multi-project persistence)? Will some current-MVP feature migrate behind a paywall later? The document is silent — deliberately, per §8, "so it doesn't compromise the MVP-phase free positioning" — but silence here isn't neutral: it's a live reputational risk (the exact bait-and-switch pattern the Vision uses to criticize a competitor) that appears nowhere in Non-Goals, Constraints, or Success Metrics as a risk to manage.

## 5. "Engineers as secondary persona" is incoherent against the Non-Goals

JTBD-4 recruits engineers explicitly: "a fast, no-friction way to run a quick 2D check... so I don't need to open (or pay for) a full professional package for a five-minute check." Meanwhile:

- §2.2 Non-Users explicitly excludes "practicing engineers needing stamped/certified deliverables for real, liability-bearing projects."
- Constraints and Guardrails requires the product to *persistently* communicate in-product that it is not for professional use, specifically because "engineers are a real secondary audience who could otherwise misapply outputs."

The document never defines where "a quick 2D check" (invited) ends and "real, liability-bearing project" (forbidden) begins. In practice, a working engineer's "quick sanity check" of a beam is almost always *in service of* a real project — informing a design decision, a client conversation, or a professional judgment call — which is precisely the liability-bearing context the Non-Goal disclaims. The PRD recruits a persona with one hand and legally disclaims that exact persona's use case with the other, without ever drawing the line that would make both statements simultaneously true. As written, either the JTBD is describing a use case the product is contractually forbidden to satisfy, or the Non-Goal is unenforceable boilerplate that any engineer will ignore the moment the tool is fast enough to be useful.

There's also no answer to the more basic question: why would a working engineer — who by definition has employer access to SAP2000/ETABS/RISA-3D, already knows those tools, and (per the competitive digest itself) has ClearCalcs as a paid transparent-report alternative built *for* them — bother opening a homework-flavored tool (AISC W-shapes + basic rectangular concrete only, no code-checking, capped at ~50 elements, explicitly labeled a learning toy) for professional work? No evidence is offered that this persona's actual blocker is cost or UI friction rather than capability (no code-checking, no member design, no 3D, no dynamic/seismic). The secondary persona is asserted, not motivated.

## 6. Unearned-confidence claims not backed by evidence in the document

- **"a student trusts its diagrams enough to check their own hand calculations against it"** (§1) — stated as strukt's bar for success. Unmeasured (see Finding 3). No user research or pilot cited to suggest trust is achievable at MVP correctness levels, let alone likely.
- **"strukt fills that gap"** (§1) — presented as a settled conclusion from a same-day, single-pass competitive digest (addendum, "Researched 2026-08-30 via web fan-out"). This is desk research assembled in one day from pricing pages and forum threads, not user validation, yet it's promoted to an unqualified claim in the PRD's Vision.
- **"a real differentiator" / "a genuine point of difference"** (addendum, Show Your Work) — grounded entirely in "none of the researched competitors expose their solve mechanics," i.e., an absence-of-feature argument. Absence of a feature among competitors is evidence the feature is hard to build or unwanted, at least as much as evidence it's an untapped opportunity — the addendum offers nothing to distinguish these two explanations. Notably, FR-16's own design ("off by default so the fast path stays fast") tacitly concedes that speed, not step-by-step derivation, is the dominant need for the primary flow — which undercuts the "real differentiator" framing rather than supporting it.
- **"The only competitor with a deliberate student program"** (SkyCiv, addendum) is used to support strukt's differentiation, but logically it's evidence *against* differentiation: the closest analog to strukt's target persona has already been captured as a line of business by a funded incumbent, and strukt's answer to that is price, not insight.
- **"genuinely teaching-oriented, not just another calculator"** (§1) — asserted as a contrast with the free calculators, but nothing in the FRs distinguishes strukt's core loop (draw → assign → load → solve → view diagrams) from those calculators' loops in any teaching-relevant way *except* the opt-in, off-by-default Show Steps toggle — which is the one feature whose adoption is an unmeasured, non-primary, non-gated metric (Finding 3).

## Additional findings (scope/spec gaps, not requested but surfaced in review)

- **FR-2 / Beam-as-Frame-preset creates a pedagogy consistency risk.** The Vision's opening line ("draws a beam, frame, or truss") presents Beam as a first-class structure choice, but the data model treats it as a UI-level constraint over Frame (`[ASSUMPTION]`, FR-2). Show Your Work (§4.7) derives from the underlying Frame stiffness matrices — nothing in the PRD addresses whether a student who explicitly picked "Beam" will be shown "Frame" terminology/matrices in the derivation view, which is exactly the kind of inconsistency that would undermine trust in a tool whose entire premise is pedagogical transparency.
- **The ~50 node/element scale limit (§2.2, §5) has no defined behavior at the boundary.** FR-11's stated principle — "never a crash or garbage output... always an explicit error" — is not extended to the scale Non-Goal. What happens at node 51? Silent slowdown, silent wrong answer, or a blocking error? Undefined, and the obvious failure mode (silent degradation) directly violates the reliability bar the PRD sets for itself elsewhere.
- **Benchmark suite sizing/ownership is unspecified.** "Maintained regression suite" (Cross-Cutting NFRs, FR-11) and SM-4's 100% pass target have no stated problem count, authorship/validation process, or maintenance owner. A 100% pass rate against an unsized, presumably self-authored suite is a metric that cannot fail by construction.
- **Cross-section catalog and unit systems may not be mutually consistent.** FR-6 scopes the catalog to AISC W-shapes (natively imperial-labeled) and basic concrete shapes; FR-20 promises SI and Imperial as fully interchangeable per-project unit systems. Nothing states whether the catalog itself converts cleanly, or whether SI-mode users get a materially different (or awkwardly converted) catalog experience.
- **Accounts/cloud persistence (FR-21–24) is scoped at full production complexity** (email/password + social login, account deletion semantics, secure password handling) with no JTBD explicitly requesting cross-session cloud save — it's inferred, not evidenced, and it's one of the more removable chunks of an already-oversized MVP (Finding 2).

---

**Net read:** this PRD tells a coherent story but validates almost none of it. The competitive claim is a same-day desk-research conjunction; the MVP is sized like a 1.0; the metrics that would actually test the thesis are secondary and ungated while the primary metrics can go green in a world where the thesis is false; the secondary persona is invited by the Vision and evicted by the Non-Goals in the same document without reconciliation; and several of the document's most confident sentences ("fills that gap," "real differentiator," "will trust its diagrams") rest on absence-of-competitor-feature arguments or unmeasured aspirations rather than anything a skeptical stakeholder would accept as evidence.
